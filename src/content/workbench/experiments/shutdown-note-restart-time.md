<p class="standfirst">Stopping work cleanly is partly an information problem: tomorrow's version of me lacks the context that feels obvious today.</p>

## Hypothesis

Writing a three-line shutdown note will shorten the time between opening the laptop and making the first meaningful change the following morning.

## Protocol

At the end of each working day, the note records:

    Current state
    Next concrete action
    One unresolved question

For ten working days, I recorded the approximate restart time and whether I changed direction before lunch.

## Results

Restart time appeared lower on complex engineering work, especially when the next action named a file, decision or person. It made little difference on days dominated by meetings or reactive work.

The measurement was too subjective. “First meaningful change” shifted depending on the kind of work, and knowing that I was timing the restart changed my behaviour.

## Conclusion

The result is inconclusive, but the practice is cheap and the qualitative benefit is obvious enough to retain. A better follow-up would measure abandoned restarts rather than elapsed minutes.

## Decision

Keep the shutdown note, drop the stopwatch, and review whether unresolved questions are answered earlier the following day.
